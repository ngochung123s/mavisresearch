American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) Clinical Practice Guideline
Title: Evaluation and Management of Well-Appearing Febrile Infants 8 to 60 Days Old
Authors: Robert H. Pantell, et al.
Journal: Pediatrics. 2021;148(2):e2021052228. PMID: 34281996. DOI: 10.1542/peds.2021-052228.

Key Action Statements:
1. Clinicians should evaluate well-appearing infants 8 to 60 days of age with fever ≥38.0°C by stratifying into 3 age groups: 8 to 21 days, 22 to 28 days, and 29 to 60 days.
2. For infants 8 to 21 days old, clinicians should obtain a complete urinalysis, blood culture, inflammatory markers, and perform lumbar puncture with cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analysis and culture, and hospitalize for parenteral antimicrobial therapy.
3. For well-appearing febrile infants 22 to 28 days old, inflammatory markers (procalcitonin, CRP, or ANC) along with urinalysis should guide the decision for lumbar puncture and hospitalization.
4. For well-appearing febrile infants 29 to 60 days old with normal inflammatory markers and negative urinalysis, outpatient management without empiric antibiotics is safe with close follow-up.
