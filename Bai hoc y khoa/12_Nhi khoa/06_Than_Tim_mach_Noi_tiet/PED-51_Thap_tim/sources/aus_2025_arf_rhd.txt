Key Statements (verbatim from full text, Table 6.3 Australian criteria for ARF diagnosis):
1. Definite initial episode of ARF: 2 major manifestations + evidence of preceding Strep A infection, or 1 major + 2 minor manifestations + evidence of preceding Strep A infection.
2. Definite recurrent episode of ARF in a patient with a documented history of ARF or RHD: 2 major manifestations + evidence of preceding Strep A infection, or 1 major + 2 minor manifestations + evidence of preceding Strep A infection, or 3 minor manifestations + evidence of a preceding Strep A infection.
3. Major manifestations (high-risk groups): Carditis (including subclinical evidence of rheumatic valvulitis on echocardiogram); Polyarthritis or aseptic monoarthritis or polyarthralgia; Sydenham chorea; Erythema marginatum; Subcutaneous nodules.
4. Major manifestations (low-risk groups): Carditis (including subclinical evidence of rheumatic valvulitis on echocardiogram); Polyarthritis; Sydenham chorea; Erythema marginatum; Subcutaneous nodules.
5. Minor manifestations (high-risk groups): Fever >=38C; Monoarthralgia; ESR >=30 mm/h or CRP >=30 mg/L; Prolonged P-R interval or advanced conduction abnormalities on ECG.
6. Minor manifestations (low-risk groups): Fever >=38.5C; Polyarthralgia or aseptic monoarthritis; ESR >=60 mm/h or CRP >=30 mg/L; Prolonged P-R interval or advanced conduction abnormalities on ECG.
7. High-risk groups are those living in communities with high rates of ARF (incidence >30/100,000 per year in 5-14-year-olds) or RHD (all-age prevalence >2/1000).
8. Evidence of preceding Strep A infection: Elevated or rising antistreptolysin O or Anti-DNase B, or a positive throat culture or rapid antigen or nucleic acid test for preceding Strep A infection.
9. Recurrent definite, probable or possible ARF requires a time period of more than 90 days after the onset of symptoms from the previous episode of definite, probable or possible ARF.
10. Chorea does not require other manifestations or evidence of preceding Strep A infection, provided other causes of chorea are excluded.
11. If polyarthritis is present as a major manifestation, polyarthralgia or aseptic monoarthritis cannot be considered an additional minor manifestation in the same person.

Key Statements (Table 10.2 Recommended antibiotic regimens for secondary prophylaxis):
12. First line: Benzathine benzylpenicillin G (BPG) 1,200,000 units (>=20 kg) or 600,000 units (<20 kg), deep intramuscular injection, every 28 days (every 21 days for selected groups).
13. BPG given every 21 days may be considered for a) patients who have breakthrough ARF despite complete adherence to a 28-day regimen, or b) are at high risk of adverse consequences if ARF occurs (have severe RHD or a history of heart valve surgery).
14. Second line (if intramuscular route is not possible or consistently declined): Phenoxymethylpenicillin (penicillin V) 250 mg orally twice a day. Following documented severe penicillin allergy: Erythromycin 250 mg orally twice a day.

Key Statements (Table 10.3 Recommended duration of secondary prophylaxis, updated 2025):
15. Definite ARF (no cardiac involvement): Minimum of 5 years after most recent episode of ARF, or until age 21 years (whichever is longer), then reassess.
16. Definite ARF (with cardiac involvement, AV conduction abnormality): Minimum of 10 years or until age 21 (whichever is longer), then reassess.
17. Stage A RHD (<=20 years, high-risk setting): Minimum of 2 years following diagnosis, then reassess.
18. Priority 3 RHD / Stage B (documented ARF history): Minimum of 10 years after the most recent episode of ARF, or until age 21 years (whichever is longer), then reassess.
19. Priority 2 RHD / moderate Stage C (documented ARF history): Minimum of 10 years after the most recent episode of ARF or until age 35 years (whichever is longer), then reassess.
20. Priority 1 RHD / severe Stage C or Stage D (documented ARF history): Minimum of 10 years after the most recent episode of ARF or until age 40 years (whichever is longer), then reassess.

Key Statements (Table 5.3 Recommended antibiotic treatment for Strep A sore throat / tonsillitis):
21. All cases: Benzathine benzylpenicillin G (BPG) single deep intramuscular injection once (Child <10 kg: 450,000 units; 10 to <20 kg: 600,000 units; >=20 kg and Adult: 1,200,000 units).
22. Oral options for 10 days: Phenoxymethylpenicillin (Child: 15 mg/kg up to 500 mg, twice daily; Adult: 500 mg, twice daily); Cefalexin (Child: 25 mg/kg up to 1 g, twice daily); Amoxicillin (Child: 25 mg/kg up to 1 g, once daily). Azithromycin for 5 days (Child: 12 mg/kg up to 500 mg, once daily).

Key Statements (Table 7.1 Medications used for acute rheumatic fever):
23. Eradication of inciting streptococcal infection: Benzathine benzylpenicillin G (BPG) 1,200,000 units (child <20 kg: 600,000 units) IMI single dose; streptococcal infection may not be evident by the time ARF manifests (e.g. cultures often negative) but eradication therapy for possible persisting streptococci is recommended, nonetheless.
24. Initial analgesia while awaiting diagnostic confirmation (mild-moderate pain): Paracetamol 1000 mg (child 15 mg/kg) orally, 4-hourly up to a maximum of 60 mg/kg/day or 4000 mg/day; preferred initial analgesia during diagnostic uncertainty, to avoid the masking effect that anti-inflammatory use can have on migratory joint symptoms, fever and inflammatory markers.
25. Symptomatic management of arthritis/arthralgia after confirmation of ARF diagnosis: Naproxen immediate-release 250-500 mg (child 10-20 mg/kg/day) orally twice daily; or Ibuprofen 200-400 mg (child 5-10 mg/kg) orally three times daily; or Aspirin adults and children 50-60 mg/kg/day orally, in four to five divided doses, escalated up to a maximum of 80-100 mg/kg/day in four to five divided doses.
26. Symptomatic management of moderate to severe chorea: Carbamazepine 3.5 to 10 mg/kg per dose orally, twice daily; Sodium valproate 7.5 to 10 mg/kg per dose orally, twice daily; plus Prednisone/prednisolone 1 to 2 mg/kg up to a maximum of 80 mg orally, once daily or in divided doses.
27. Symptomatic management of carditis (paediatric dosing): Furosemide 1 to 2 mg/kg orally as a single dose, then 0.5 to 1 mg/kg (to a maximum of 6 mg/kg) orally, 6- to 24-hourly; Spironolactone 1 to 3 mg/kg (initially) up to 100 mg orally, daily; Enalapril 0.1 mg/kg orally, daily, increased gradually over 2 weeks to a maximum of 1 mg/kg orally, daily. Treatment of heart failure may be required in severe, acute carditis. The management of acute carditis follows the same principles as the management of acute heart failure.
28. Disease-modifying (immunomodulatory) treatments: Prednisone/prednisolone 1 to 2 mg/kg up to a maximum of 80 mg orally, once daily or in divided doses. Considered for use in selected cases of severe carditis, despite meta-analyses in which overall benefit was not evident. (GRADE 2C)
