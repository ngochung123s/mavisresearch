TITLE: Reference Ranges for Left Modified Myocardial Performance Index: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

=== ABSTRACT ===
The modified myocardial performance index (mod-MPI) is a noninvasive Doppler-derived metric used to evaluate fetal cardiac function. However, the reference ranges for mod-MPI in normal fetuses are not clearly defined, which limits the use of this technology in fetuses with potential cardiac compromise. Thus, we aimed to perform a systematic review and meta-analysis of published mod-MPI reference ranges across gestation.
The published literature was systematically searched, and all published articles in any language that provided values for the left ventricular mod-MPI obtained in low-risk, singleton fetuses were considered eligible for further review. All retrieved titles and abstracts were independently reviewed by two researchers. Mean and standard deviation by gestational week was extracted or calculated from published data. DerSimonian-Laird random-effects models were used to estimate pooled means and 95% confidence intervals (CIs).
The search resulted in 618 unique citations, of which 583 did not meet inclusion criteria, leaving 35 abstracts selected for full-text review. Review of the references of these 35 articles identified another 5 studies of interest. Of the 40 articles reviewed, six met inclusion criteria. There was significant heterogeneity seen in the mod-MPI results reported. Mod-MPI increased as pregnancy progressed in all studies. The pooled mean mod-MPI at 11 weeks’ gestation was 0.400 (95% CI 0.374–0.426) and increased to 0.585 (95% CI 0.533–0.637) at 41 weeks’ gestation. The increase was linear in 5 of 6 studies, while in 1 study, the mod-MPI was stable until 27 weeks’ gestation, and then increased throughout the third trimester. Despite all having trends increasing over pregnancy, there was no study in which all the weekly means fell within the pooled 95% CI.
While mod-MPI does increase over gestation, the true “reference ranges” for fetuses remain elusive. Future efforts to further optimize calculation of time intervals possibly via automation are desperately needed to allow for reproducibility of this potentially very useful tool to assess fetal cardiac function.
Keywords: Fetal cardiac function, Mod-MPI, Reference ranges, Echocardiography, Doppler


## INTRODUCTION
Fetal echocardiography was initially implemented to increase the prenatal detection of structural congenital heart disease (CHD) [1]. However, with advances in prenatal imaging technology, assessment of fetal cardiac function has also become an essential component of fetal echocardiography. Fetuses both with and without structural CHD can have depressed systolic and/or diastolic function and prenatal identification of this dysfunction can significantly alter pregnancy management [2].
The myocardial performance index (MPI) is a noninvasive Doppler-derived metric that can be used to evaluate cardiac function [3–5]. The MPI is the ratio between the duration of ventricular isovolumetric period (including the isovolumetric contraction time [ICT] and isovolumetric relaxation time [IRT]) and ejection time (ET) and was first proposed by Tei et al. [3] for use in adults with cardiac amyloidosis. The MPI is reproducible and unaffected by cardiac size and heart rate, and higher values have been shown to predict cardiac failure in dilated cardiomyopathy. When implemented in fetal echocardiography, an abnormal MPI was shown to predict hemodynamic deterioration in appropriately grown fetuses, perinatal mortality in growth-restricted fetuses, and severity of volume overload in the recipient twin when affected by twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome [6–9]. However, reproducibility was found to be decreased compared to the adult population, which was felt to be due to less clear landmarks in the Doppler waveforms obtained from fetal echocardiography [10].
Hernandez-Andrade et al. [10] proposed a modified left ventricular MPI (mod-MPI) utilizing the beginning of the mitral valve (MV) and aortic valve (AV) opening and closure (clicks) seen on a single Doppler waveform to improve consistency in measuring the necessary time intervals. Using the mod-MPI, the IRT is time from AV closure click to MV opening click, the ICT is the time from the MV closure click to the AV opening click, and the ET is the time from the AV opening click to closure click (shown in Fig. 1).

Most studies published since then have focused on the use of mod-MPI in situations where fetal cardiac compromise is likely, such as twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome, fetal CHD, and growth restriction [11–13]. However, the reference ranges for mod-MPI in normal fetuses are not clearly defined, which limits the use of this technology in fetuses with potential cardiac compromise. Additionally, the impact of advancing gestational age on mod-MPI is unclear. Thus, we aimed to perform a systematic review and meta-analysis of published mod-MPI reference ranges in normal fetuses across gestational ages.

## METHODS
This was a systematic review and meta-analysis conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. The study was exempt from review by the Institutional Review Board and was registered with the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO, CRD42020202247).
The published literature was searched using strategies created by a medical librarian for fetal myocardial performance index. The search strategies were implemented in Ovid Medline 1946, Embase 1947, Scopus 1823, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, and ClinicalTrials.gov and were established using a combination of standardized terms and keywords including but not limited to (fetus OR fetal heart) AND (myocardial performance index OR Tei index). The search also included a narrow search for citations that mention parts of MPI, including ejection, isovolumetric contraction, and isovolumetric relaxation time. Animal studies that did not include humans were excluded. All searches were completed in August 2020. The literature search was executed again in June 2022. Full search strategies are provided in online supplementary Material (for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000535602).
For the initial search, all published articles in any language that provided values for the left ventricular mod-MPI obtained in low-risk, singleton fetuses were considered eligible for further review. Articles that did not utilize ultrasound and that utilized only tissue Doppler imaging were excluded. Additionally, only articles that included numerical data (number of included fetuses and either means and standard deviations or medians and percentiles) stratified by gestational age in weekly increments were included.
All retrieved titles and abstracts were independently reviewed by two practicing maternal-fetal medicine physicians (K.H.B. and J.I.R.) for potential inclusion. Any disagreements were resolved by consensus. The articles selected for full-text review were then reviewed by the two investigators for eligibility to be included in the study. If the study met all inclusion criteria except for the reporting of specific numerical values by gestational week, the corresponding author of the potential study was contacted via email to request the missing data. Data from included articles and provider by authors were extracted into a database.
The quality of each study was assessed by the same two investigators. We utilized a quality assessment tool developed specifically to assess studies that report reference ranges as these studies are inherently different from investigational studies [14]. Domains assessed with the tool included the definition of “normal” for gravidas, fetuses, and pregnancy overall, quality and clarity of the measurements reported, and appropriateness of statistic tests used.
Data published as median and percentiles or as mean and variance were transformed to mean and standard deviation using standard methodology. DerSimonian-Laird random-effects models were used to estimate pooled means and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for the mod-MPI for each gestational week, as well as for the gestational age ranges: 11–16, 17–20, 21–24, 25–28, 29–32, 33–36, and ≥37 weeks’ gestation. Heterogeneity was assessed using the I2 and Cochran’s Q2 tests. All statistical analyses were performed using Stata 12 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA).

## RESULTS
There were a total of 1,232 results found by our detailed search. A total of 614 duplicates were removed, bringing the final total to 618 unique citations. Review of abstracts identified 583 studies not meeting inclusion criteria, leaving 35 abstracts selected for full-text review. Review of the references of these 35 articles identified another 5 studies of interest. These 40 studies were reviewed by two investigators (K.H.B. and J.I.R.) and 34 of them excluded from further review. Reasons for exclusion included no report of mod-MPI (n = 17), no full-text article published (n = 7), and lack of results reported by gestational age in weeks and no response to request for this information (n = 10). The remaining 6 articles were included in our meta-analysis (shown in Fig. 2; Table 1) [15–20].
Flowsheet of included studies.
Characteristics of included studies
The 6 included studies included 2,486 mod-MPI measurements in fetuses ranging in gestational age from 11 weeks’ to 41 weeks’ gestation. Each study contributed between 14 and 41% of the data. The majority of the measurements were at 27 weeks’ gestation or beyond, and only 2 studies included fetuses at 18 weeks’ gestation or earlier or 38 weeks’ gestation or later.
The quality assessment (shown in online suppl. Table 1) demonstrated poor reporting regarding maternal and pregnancy characteristics. In fact, no study reported the exclusion of women with prior stillbirth, obesity, uterine anomalies, tobacco and drug use, or other teratogen exposure. Mode of conception was not reported in any study. Outside of meeting our inclusion criteria of anatomically normal, singleton fetuses, further classification of fetuses as “low risk” was also lacking. Accelerated fetal growth was not an exclusion in two included studies, but growth restriction was excluded in all cases. Genetic screening or testing was not reported, and it is unclear if those with abnormal genetic results were excluded from any study. Also, the validation of prenatal low-risk fetuses was not confirmed by postnatal follow-up in most studies, with only two studies requiring a live birth to be included in the analysis.
All studies included a detailed description of technique for obtaining mod-MPI, and the described measurement techniques were identical in all included studies. Fetal echocardiography was performed on a variety of ultrasound machines. No studies blinded the examiner to gestational age, and only half of the included studies examined intra- and interobserver correlation, which is important in determining the reproducibility of results. One study, which enrolled patients at 27–29 and 35–37 weeks’ gestation, did not report how gestational age was assigned and also did not exclude gravidas with suboptimally dated pregnancies [18].

As seen in the meta-analysis by gestational week, there was significant heterogeneity at each of the seven gestational age ranges with I2 values ranging from 64.1% to 96.0%.

The inter-observer correlation was weak to moderate with correlation coefficients of 0.08–0.43. The intra-observer correlation was moderate to high with correlation coefficients of 0.58–0.80.

## REPRODUCIBILITY
Only one study each reported on the inter-observer reproducibility, while two studies reported on the intra-observer reproducibility of mod-MPI measurements [17, 18, 20]. The inter-observer correlation was weak to moderate with correlation coefficients of 0.08–0.43. The intra-observer correlation was moderate to high with correlation coefficients of 0.58–0.80.

## DISCUSSION
This systematic review and meta-analysis of reported reference ranges for fetal mod-MPI across gestation by week included 6 studies. All studies showed an increase in mod-MPI over pregnancy; however, whether that increase is linear over the course of pregnancy or initially stable with a steeper increase in the third trimester remains unclear. Additionally, the true “normal” range remains elusive as there was considerable heterogeneity in the results published. This is crucial for clinicians to understand as it is unclear which, if any, previously published ranges can be utilized in a different population.
The increase in mod-MPI over gestation is also consistent with most manuscripts reporting left mod-MPI reference ranges but not meeting our inclusion criteria [21–23]. However, a few other studies reported either a negative or no correlation between mod-MPI and gestational age [24, 25]. As in the included studies, the absolute values of mod-MPI in excluded studies were heterogenous and differed from our pooled data. One of the largest excluded studies by Hernandez-Andrade et al. [21] reported reference ranges on 557 normal fetuses and found the mean mod-MPI increased from 0.35 at 19 weeks’ to 0.37 at 39 weeks’ gestation. This is in contrast to our means of 0.462 (95% CI 0.407–0.518) at 19 weeks’ and 0.543 (95% CI 0.500–0.586) at 39 weeks’ gestation.
Despite these findings, we feel the mod-MPI shows significant promise in assessing fetal cardiac function. In many situations, the combined cardiac output (CCO), which is the sum of the LV and RV stroke volumes, has been the historical standard of care for fetal cardiac function [26]. However, there are many limitations to the CCO. The calculation of stroke volume relies on accurate measurement of diameter of the outflow valve and velocity of blood flow, which can be technically difficult to obtain. Also, the equation for CCO assumes that the outflow valves are circular and the vessels are noncompliant [27].
The mod-MPI is easier to obtain technically and has already been modified to adjust for inaccuracy in time measurements by utilizing the valve opening and closure clicks [10]. However, despite this modification, our results clearly demonstrate that reproducibility of this test is fraught with error. Not only are the results from the different studies heterogeneous with regard to the actual mean and standard deviation of mod-MPI at each gestational week, but the reported inter-observer reproducibility was also subpar when reported [18, 20]. While the intra-observer reproducibility is high in one included study, the other included study reported lower correlation within assessments done by the same examiner [16, 20]. We hypothesize that the heterogeneity between publications identified by our meta-analysis and the reported suboptimal reproducibility is mainly due to small differences in identifying the valve clicks. As the isovolumetric time measurements are in milliseconds, small differences in caliper placement may have large impacts on the calculated mod-MPI. This is highlighted in the study by Meriki et al. [16] where using three different methods to calculate the mod-MPI (“original”: from the beginning of the clicks on the opposite side of the waveform, “reflected”: from the beginning of the clicks on the same side of the waveform, and “peak”: from the peak of the clicks) yielded three differing results for the mod-MPI. Likewise, changes in machine settings have shown to impact reproducibility of the mod-MPI [28]. In order to address this, recent studies have investigated the use of automation to limit errors and have shown promising results, including reproducibility of close to 100% [29–34]. However, all the published literature regarding automation of fetal mod-MPI comes from one group, which may reflect the limitations in widespread utilization of automation for mod-MPI.
Our study is strengthened by a robust and rigorous systematic review of the literature with the guidance of a trained research librarian. Authors of studies, which did not report data by week, were contacted so as to include all available published data in our meta-analysis. We also assessed the quality of each included study, which allowed us to assess whether each study truly included only “normal” fetuses. However, the study is not without limitation. Despite our attempts to contact authors of previously published papers, there were still 10 studies excluded as the mod-MPI results were unavailable by gestational week. Despite each study describing its included participants as “low risk,” the actual description of inclusion criteria is lacking in all studies. Thus, it is possible that fetuses with or at risk of cardiac compromise were included in any included study. Lastly, while no one study contributed over 41% of the overall data, there were minimal data available at early and late gestational ages, leaving only 1 or 2 studies contributing data for 12 of the 31 examined gestational weeks.
We found that while mod-MPI does increase over the course of pregnancy, the true “reference ranges” for fetuses during each gestational week remain elusive. While we do report a pooled mean and 95% CI by gestational week, these reference ranges should be utilized with caution as none of the means from the individual studies would all fall within our reference range due to significant heterogeneity in previously reported results. Future efforts to further optimize calculation of time intervals possibly via automation are desperately needed to allow for reproducibility of this potentially very useful tool to assess fetal cardiac function.
