TITLE: Bioequivalence of a Fixed‐Dose Combination of Relugolix, Estradiol, and Norethindrone Acetate: An Open‐Label, Randomized, Fully Replicate Crossover Study in Healthy Postmenopausal Women Under Fasting Conditions

=== ABSTRACT ===
Uterine fibroids are the most common benign gynecological tumors, often causing heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB), anemia, and impaired quality of life. A fixed‐dose combination (FDC) of relugolix (40 mg), estradiol (1 mg), and norethindrone acetate (0.5 mg) was developed to address these limitations. This open‐label, randomized, two‐treatment, two‐sequence, four‐period, crossover bioequivalence study was conducted in 48 healthy postmenopausal women under fasting conditions. Participants received a single oral dose of either the RELUEASE (Sun Pharmaceutical Industries Limited, India) or the reference product (MYFEMBREE) (Patheon Inc., Mississauga, Ontario), with a 21‐day washout. Pharmacokinetics parameters were derived using non‐compartmental analysis. Bioequivalence assessment was performed using a linear mixed‐effects model applied to ln‐transformed pharmacokinetic parameters. A total of 39 participants completed all study periods. The point estimates and 90% confidence intervals (CIs), or the 95% upper bound for the pharmacokinetic parameters (Cmax and AUC) of relugolix, estradiol, norethindrone, and estrone met the predefined bioequivalence acceptance criteria. Six adverse events were reported, all mild, transient in nature, and resolving without sequelae. No clinically significant safety concerns were observed during the study. The relugolix/estradiol/norethindrone acetate FDC demonstrated bioequivalence to the globally approved reference product (MYFEMBREE) with comparable tolerability. This provides a safe, effective, reversible non‐surgical therapy for fibroid‐related HMB, fulfilling an unmet need beyond surgery.
Keywords: bioequivalence, estradiol, leiomyoma, norethindrone acetate, relugolix, uterine fibroids


## MATERIALS AND METHODS
This was a Phase I, open‐label, randomized, balanced, two‐treatment, two‐sequence, four‐period, fully replicate crossover bioavailability study conducted under fasting conditions. Each subject received both the Test RELUEASE or the reference product (MYFEMBREE) in a randomized sequence across four study periods, allowing within‐subject comparison of pharmacokinetic (PK) parameters and estimation of intra‐subject variability.
This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, relevant USFDA guidelines, and the International Council for Harmonisation‐Good Clinical Practice (ICH‐GCP) E6(R3) guidelines. It also complied with the local regulatory requirements for Good Clinical Practice in India (Good Clinical Practices for Clinical Research in India, 2004; Central Drugs Standard Control Organization [CDSCO]); the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019; and the Indian Council of Medical Research's National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research Involving Human Participants (2017). Written approval was obtained from the site Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) (IEC No:IECSSH050722), and informed consent was secured from all study participants. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before screening.

## TREATMENT SEQUENCES AND STUDY DURATION
Subjects were randomized equally into one of the following two treatment sequences.
The total duration of the clinical phase was 69 days, calculated from the check‐in of the first period to the completion of the fourth period. This was a four‐period crossover study design. Subjects were housed at the clinical facility at least 36 h prior to dosing and remained confined for a minimum of 72 h post‐dose in each study period.
A washout interval of at least 21 days (≥5 half‐lives of the analytes) was maintained between consecutive dosing periods to prevent carryover effects.
Healthy, non‐smoking postmenopausal women aged 40‐70 years with a body mass index (BMI) between 18.5 and 30.0 kg/m2 and serum estradiol concentrations <30 pg/mL at screening were enrolled. Postmenopausal status was confirmed based on medical history and hormonal profile. Subjects were excluded if they had any significant medical illness, abnormal clinical findings, known hypersensitivity to study drugs, recent participation in another clinical study within 90 days before screening, or use of medications known to interfere with study drug metabolism.
This study was conducted to compare the rate and extent of absorption of relugolix, estradiol, and norethindrone acetate tablets (40/1/0.5 mg) manufactured by Sun Pharmaceutical Industries Limited, India (Test product), that is, RELUEASE, with MYFEMBREE (relugolix, estradiol, and norethindrone acetate tablets, 40/1/0.5 mg) manufactured by Patheon Inc., 2100 Syntex Court, Mississauga, Ontario L5N 7K9, Canada, and distributed by Myovant Sciences, Inc., Brisbane (Reference product), in healthy postmenopausal female subjects under fasting conditions.

## STUDY PROCEDURE
The Test product was RELUEASE FDC tablet containing relugolix 40 mg, estradiol 1 mg, and norethindrone acetate 0.5 mg (Sun Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd., India).The Reference product was MYFEMBREE (relugolix/estradiol/norethindrone acetate, 40/1/0.5 mg) manufactured by Patheon Inc. (Canada) and distributed by Myovant Sciences (USA).
Each subject received a single oral dose of one tablet (Test or Reference) with 240 mL of water under fasting conditions (≥10 h overnight fast) in each study period. Standardized meals were provided 4 h post‐dose.
Subjects who met all eligibility criteria based on the inclusion and exclusion parameters were randomly assigned to receive the study products. Randomization was performed using the PROC PLAN procedure in SAS (Version 9.4, SAS Institute Inc., USA) in a block design to ensure balance across sequences. The allocation of Test (T) and Reference (R) formulations for each subject during the four study periods was determined as per the randomization schedule, ensuring equal distribution of subjects across all treatment sequences.
Venous blood samples (5 mL each) were collected from a forearm vein into pre‐labeled tubes at 22 time points per period: pre‐dose at −1.0, −0.5, and 0 h, and post‐dose at 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.33, 1.67, 2.00, 2.50, 3.00, 4.00, 5.00, 6.00, 8.00, 11.00, 12.00, 16.00, 24.00, 48.00, and 72.00 h post‐dose in each period. Plasma was separated by centrifugation and stored at −50°C until analysis. Plasma concentrations of relugolix, norethindrone, estradiol (baseline corrected and uncorrected), and estrone (baseline corrected and uncorrected) were determined using a validated LC‐MS/MS method with appropriate lower limits of quantification, accuracy, and precision.
The bioanalytical method was validated in accordance with applicable regulatory guidelines, including those recommended by the US FDA and the EMA, which are widely adopted in bioequivalence studies conducted in India. Supplementary file 1 gives the bioanalytical condition used for estimation by UPLC‐MS/MS
Blood samples were collected via an indwelling intravenous cannula inserted into a forearm vein prior to pre‐dose sampling. All samples were collected into pre‐labeled vacutainers (indicating project number, subject number, period number, and sampling time point) containing K3EDTA as an anticoagulant. The blood samples were then centrifuged at 3300 RPM for 15 min under refrigeration to separate plasma for analysis.
Descriptive statistics, including arithmetic mean, minimum, maximum, standard deviation, and coefficient of variation, were calculated for all demographic variables.
Pharmacokinetic (PK) and statistical analyses were performed using SAS software (Version 9.4, SAS Institute Inc., USA), following the Draft Guidance for Industry: Bioequivalence Studies with Pharmacokinetic Endpoints for Drugs Submitted Under an ANDA (US FDA, August 2021). Individual PK parameters for relugolix, norethindrone, and baseline‐corrected total estrone were ln‐transformed prior to analysis.
Baseline concentrations correction was done subject and period specific basis. The mean of the three pre‐dose plasma concentrations measured at −1.000, −0.500, and 0.000 h was calculated and subtracted from the pre‐dose (0.000 h) and all subsequent post‐dose concentrations. Any negative concentration values obtained following baseline correction were set to zero for all pharmacokinetic evaluations.
The within‐subject standard deviation of the reference product (σWR) was estimated using a mixed‐model ANOVA (PROC MIXED) applied to differences between ln‐transformed values of the reference product, with Sequence as a fixed effect.
If σWR ≥ 0.294, indicating high within‐subject variability, a reference‐scaled average bioequivalence (RSABE) approach was applied. In this case, ln‐transformed parameters (Cmax, AUC0‐t, AUC0‐∞, and AUC0‐72 h) were analyzed using mixed‐model ANOVA using Sequence effect as a fixed effect to estimate least‐square means (LSM), standard errors, and the upper confidence bound associated with the Test–Reference difference.
If σWR < 0.294, a conventional average bioequivalence (ABE) approach was used. The ln‐transformed PK parameters were analyzed using mixed‐model ANOVA with Sequence, Period, and Treatment as fixed effects and appropriate random and repeated effects. The Sequence effect was tested at a 10% significance level, and all other main effects at 5%.
All analyses were performed using PROC MIXED (SAS Version 9.4).

## ENDPOINTS FOR THE STUDY
Primary PK parameters included maximum plasma concentration (Cmax), area under the plasma concentration–time curve from time zero to the last quantifiable concentration (AUC0‐t), and area under the plasma concentration–time curve extrapolated to infinity (AUC0‐∞).
Secondary parameters included time to reach Cmax (Tmax), terminal elimination rate constant (Kel), terminal elimination half‐life (t_½), and the percentage of extrapolated AUC.
Bioequivalence was concluded if the 90% confidence intervals (CIs) for the geometric mean ratios (GMRS) of Cmax, AUC0‐t, AUC0‐∞, and AUC0‐72 h (for relugolix only) within the predefined acceptance range of 80%‐125%, or, in cases of high variability (reference CV >30%), if the 95% upper bound of the reference‐scaled approach was ≤0.
Safety was evaluated through monitoring of adverse events (AEs), clinical laboratory investigations (hematology, biochemistry, and urinalysis), vital signs (blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, and temperature), 12‐lead electrocardiograms (ECG), and chest X‐rays. All AEs were recorded, assessed for severity and relationship to study drug, and followed until resolution.
No concomitant medication was permitted during the restricted period as detailed below. Subjects abstained from taking any prescribed medications, including herbal preparations and vitamin supplements, for at least 2 weeks prior to study initiation and throughout the study duration. Similarly, the use of over‐the‐counter (OTC) medications, herbal remedies, or vitamin supplements was prohibited for at least 1 week prior to study initiation and until study completion.
Considering a Test/Reference ratio up to 95%‐105% and intra‐subject CV of approximately 38%, 80 observations (40 Subjects) per treatment had yielded a power of 90% to meet 80%‐125% acceptance criteria. However, to be conservative and to allow for possible dropouts and/or withdrawals, 96 observations per treatment (48 subjects) were considered for this fully replicate crossover study.

## RESULTS
A total of 48 healthy postmenopausal women were enrolled and randomized. Of these, 39 subjects completed all four study periods. Nine subjects discontinued: six non‐compliance to protocol requirement of non‐participation in another trial within 90 days, one positive for drug abuse, one due to adverse events (AEs), and one for personal reasons. ## PHARMACOKINETIC ANALYSIS
The pharmacokinetic parameters, Cmax, AUC0‐t, AUC0‐∞, and AUC0‐72 were analyzed for relugolix, norethindrone, unconjugated estradiol (baseline corrected and uncorrected), unconjugated estrone (baseline corrected and uncorrected), and total estrone (baseline corrected and uncorrected).
The pharmacokinetic profiles of relugolix, estradiol, norethindrone, and estrone were comparable between the test and reference formulations.
For relugolix, the GMR (Test/Reference) for Cmax was 93.24%, and for AUC0‐72 was 98.89% (90% CI: 90.86‐107.62).
For estradiol, the GMRs for Cmax, AUC0‐t, and AUC0‐∞ were 93.21% (90% CI: 87.38‐99.43), 99.74% (95% upper bound: −0.09), and 98.73% (95% upper bound: −0.10), respectively.
For norethindrone, the GMRs were 108.16% (90% CI: 102.20‐114.48) for Cmax, 94.88% (90% CI: 92.01‐97.85) for AUC0‐t, and 95.72% (90% CI: 93.30‐98.20) for AUC0‐∞.
For total estrone, the GMRs for Cmax, AUC0‐t, and AUC0‐∞ were 118.62% (90% CI: 112.90‐124.63), 100.47% (90% CI: 97.34‐103.69), and 99.77% (90% CI: 96.88‐102.74), respectively.
For parameters evaluated using the RSABE approach, including selected parameters for relugolix, estradiol, and free estrone, the 95% upper bounds of the scaled bioequivalence metric were within the regulatory acceptance criteria of ≤0.
Overall, the 90% confidence intervals for pharmacokinetic parameters evaluated using the ABE approach were within the predefined acceptance range of 80%‐125%, demonstrating bioequivalence between the test and reference formulations (Table 2); refer to Figures 2, 3, 4.

## DISCUSSION
The present study demonstrated bioequivalence between the test FDC RELUEASE (Sun Pharmaceutical Industries Limited, India) and the reference product (MYFEMBREE) (Patheon Inc. 2100 Syntex Court, Mississauga, Ontario L5N 7K9 Canada).
The 90% confidence intervals (CIs) for the GMRs of Cmax, AUC0‐t, AUC0‐∞, and AUC0‐72 for RELUEASE were within the predefined acceptance range of 80%‐125%. Furthermore, when the RSABE approach was applied (reference CV >30%), the 95% upper bound was ≤0 for applicable pharmacokinetic parameters, meeting the regulatory acceptance criteria.
The test and reference products were demonstrated to be bioequivalent based on pharmacokinetic parameters, supporting their therapeutic equivalence.
The clinical relevance of these findings is underscored by the robust efficacy and safety data generated in pivotal Phase 3 programs. The LIBERTY 1 and LIBERTY 2 trials, along with their extension study, demonstrated that the relugolix‐based FDC significantly reduced HMB (P < .0001), improved anemia (P < .001), and preserved BMD during treatment periods of up to 24 months. 13 , 14 Collectively, these studies established the clinical efficacy and safety of the FDC, leading to its approval by the US FDA and the EMA for up to 24 months of use in women with HMB associated with uterine fibroids. 15 , 16 , 17
In our study, relugolix exhibits a complex pharmacokinetic profile characterized by a double peak phenomenon in its plasma concentration–time profile following oral administration. 18 , 19 Our study showed similar pattern for reference and test product. The drug is rapidly absorbed, with an initial peak often observed as early as 0.5 h post‐dose, followed by one or more subsequent absorption peaks that lead to a median of approximately 2.25 h. 18 , 20 This multiple peaking behavior is primarily attributed to the drug's role as a substrate for the intestinal efflux transporter P‐glycoprotein (P‐gp), which serves as a major determinant of its absorption and low absolute bioavailability of approximately 12%. 18 , 21 The phenomenon may also be influenced by physiological factors such as irregular gastric emptying, which can result in discrete “windows” of absorption as the drug moves through different sections of the gastrointestinal tract. 19 , 22 Despite this variability in absorption, the overall exposure and clinical efficacy of relugolix remain consistent, and it can be administered without regard to food intake. 18 , 20
The availability of a bioequivalent formulation is particularly significant in addressing the unmet medical need for non‐surgical management of uterine fibroids. Surgical interventions such as hysterectomy, myomectomy, and uterine artery embolization remain widely used but are associated with risks, recovery burden, and recurrence rates that are especially problematic for women desiring uterine preservation. 3 , 4 While medical therapies, including GnRH agonists and estrogen/progestin regimens, can provide symptomatic relief, their utility is constrained by hypoestrogenic side effects or suboptimal efficacy in fibroid‐driven bleeding. 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 In contrast, the relugolix‐based FDC combines potent GnRH receptor antagonism with add‐back therapy, balancing efficacy with long‐term tolerability.
While the initial study focused on pharmacokinetic evaluation rather than direct clinical endpoints, this was an appropriate strategy to confirm bioequivalence between formulations. In addition, a Phase 3 randomized, multi‐center clinical trial (REEMEMBER; Clinical Trial Registry of India: CTRI/2024/06/068615) has been initiated to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the relugolix‐based FDC in comparison with leuprolide in women with HMB associated with uterine fibroids. 23
Taken together, these findings support the relugolix/estradiol/norethindrone acetate FDC as a pivotal addition to the fibroid treatment landscape. It offers a reversible, non‐surgical, once‐daily oral option that bridges the gap between invasive surgical approaches and inadequate medical monotherapies.
RELUEASE (Sun Pharmaceutical Industries Limited, India) has been developed as a generic formulation of this FDC. In line with regulatory requirements for generic drug approval, demonstration of pharmacokinetic bioequivalence to the reference product forms the primary basis for establishing therapeutic equivalence.

## CONCLUSION
The RELUEASE (Sun Pharmaceutical Industries Limited, India) FDC (relugolix, estradiol, and norethindrone acetate) was found to be pharmacokinetically bioequivalent to the reference product MYFEMBREE (Patheon Inc. 2100 Syntex Court, Mississauga, Ontario L5N 7K9 Canada) and was safe and well tolerated in healthy postmenopausal women. This FDC offers a convenient, reversible, non‐surgical option for managing HMB associated with uterine fibroids while minimizing bone loss and improving tolerability.
